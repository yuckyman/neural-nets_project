The Cookie Cats dataset captures an A/B test in a mobile puzzle game, focusing on how gate placement affects short- and medium-term retention. Each record is a unique player (`userid`), labeled with the gate variant they saw (`version`: `gate_30` earlier vs `gate_40` later), their early play intensity (`sum_gamerounds` across the first 7 days), and two binary retention signals: returned the next day (`retention_1`) and returned within a week (`retention_7`). There are 90,189 rows, split roughly evenly between gate variants, giving decent power for uplift analysis.

Baseline retention is ~44.5% on day 1 and ~18.6% on day 7. The `gate_30` variant shows a small uplift: D1 ≈ 0.448 vs 0.442, D7 ≈ 0.190 vs 0.182. Early engagement is a strong leading indicator; players in higher `sum_gamerounds` deciles come back more often, which makes that feature useful both for understanding heterogeneity (does the gate help only among highly engaged players?) and for building simple propensity or uplift models. Because all fields are already clean and typed (categorical version + numeric rounds + binary labels), you can jump straight into train/validation splits, stratified on version to preserve treatment balance.

Operationally, the raw CSV lives at `data/cookie_cats/raw.csv`; a cleaned Parquet is ready at `data/cookie_cats/clean.parquet`. Existing EDA visuals in `reports/figures` show overall retention, uplift by gate, and retention vs early engagement—handy for quick stakeholder readouts. With the newly added `models/` notebooks, you now have starting points for logistic baselines and simple neural nets; both can be fed directly from the cleaned Parquet without extra preprocessing. Overall, it’s a tidy, lightweight table suited for fast experimentation on retention and treatment effects.
